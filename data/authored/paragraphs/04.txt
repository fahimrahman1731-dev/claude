=== p030 | Artificial Intelligence at Work | technology | A
Artificial intelligence is rapidly changing the way many companies operate. Programs can now analyze huge amounts of data in seconds, identify patterns and even predict which products customers will want. In hospitals, software helps doctors examine medical images and detect illness earlier than before. However, critics warn that automation may lead to the displacement of workers whose tasks can be done by machines. There are also serious ethical questions about privacy and bias in the systems. Most experts agree that governments must develop clear regulations. The challenge is to gain the benefits without ignoring the risks.

=== p031 | The Industrial Revolution | history | A
The Industrial Revolution began in Britain in the late eighteenth century and gradually spread across Europe and North America. New machines powered by steam allowed factories to produce goods far more quickly and cheaply than skilled workers could by hand. As a result, millions of people moved from villages to rapidly growing cities in search of employment. Working conditions in the early factories were often dangerous, and even young children worked long hours. Over time, reformers campaigned for laws to protect workers and improve housing. The period transformed not only the economy but also the structure of society.

=== p032 | Migration of Birds | nature | A
Every autumn, millions of birds leave their breeding grounds and travel thousands of kilometers to warmer regions. Scientists have long been fascinated by how these birds find their way across oceans and deserts. Research suggests that they use several methods, including the position of the sun, the pattern of the stars and the magnetic field of the earth. Some species fly without stopping for days, while others rest and feed at familiar locations along the route. Climate change and the loss of wetlands now threaten many of these ancient journeys. Protecting key habitats is therefore essential.

=== p033 | The Value of Critical Thinking | education | A
In an age of endless online information, critical thinking has become an essential skill. Students are constantly exposed to articles, videos and advertisements that may contain misleading or incomplete claims. Critical thinkers do not simply accept what they read; instead, they evaluate the source, look for evidence and consider alternative explanations. They are also willing to change their opinions when new information appears. Many universities now include courses designed to develop these abilities. Employers also value graduates who can analyze problems carefully. Such skills will remain important regardless of future technology.

=== p034 | Urban Gardens | environment | A
In many crowded cities, residents are transforming empty lots and rooftops into small community gardens. These urban gardens provide fresh vegetables for families who might otherwise struggle to afford healthy food. They also create green spaces where neighbors can meet, share knowledge and build stronger relationships. Researchers have found that gardening can reduce stress and improve mental health. In addition, plants help to cool the surrounding streets during the hot summer months. Some city councils now offer free seeds and training to encourage new projects. The movement shows how small local actions can have significant benefits.

=== p035 | The Mystery of the Ancient Stones | history | A
On a quiet plain in the north of the country stands a circle of enormous stones. Archaeologists believe the monument was constructed more than four thousand years ago, although its exact purpose remains uncertain. Some of the stones weigh as much as twenty tons and were transported from hills over two hundred kilometers away. Several theories have been proposed to explain the structure. One suggests that it served as a calendar, because the stones align with the sunrise on the longest day of the year. Others argue that it was a place of ceremony and burial. Further research may one day reveal the answer.

=== p036 | Globalization and Local Culture | society | A
Globalization has connected economies and cultures more closely than ever before. Today, people in different continents watch the same films, wear similar clothes and eat at the same international restaurants. Supporters argue that this exchange spreads ideas, creates jobs and reduces poverty in developing countries. However, critics worry that local languages, crafts and traditions are slowly disappearing. Small producers often find it difficult to compete with powerful multinational companies. In response, some communities have started programs to preserve their heritage. Finding a balance between openness and identity remains a major challenge.

=== p037 | The Silence Between Us | story | A
My mother was never a woman who shared her emotions freely. Even when I was a child, she showed her love through actions rather than words. She cooked my favorite meals, mended my torn clothes and waited at the window until I came home from school. When I moved to another country, the distance between us seemed to grow, and our phone calls became short and polite. Last winter, I returned home unexpectedly and found her reading my old letters. She looked up, and for the first time in years she smiled without hesitation. In that moment, the silence finally broke.

=== p038 | Plastic in the Oceans | environment | A
Every year, millions of tons of plastic waste end up in the world's oceans. Much of it comes from rivers that carry rubbish from cities and farms out to sea. Over time, sunlight and waves break the plastic into tiny pieces that are almost invisible. Fish and seabirds often mistake these particles for food, and the plastic can then enter the human food chain. Cleaning the oceans is extremely difficult because the waste is spread across vast areas. Many experts believe the most effective solution is to reduce plastic use at the source. Several countries have already banned single-use bags.

=== p039 | The Escape from the Outpost | science fiction | A
The power surges began without warning on the fortieth day of the mission. Lights flickered across the outpost, and several systems failed one after another. The crew quickly realized that they could not repair the main generator with the equipment on board. They rationed their remaining food and water while the commander calculated a route back to the ship. Exhaustion and uncertainty made every decision feel dangerous. On the third night, they finally picked up a faint signal from the rescue vessel. Within hours, they were climbing aboard, tired but alive.

=== p040 | Working from Home | work | I
Since the pandemic, many companies have allowed employees to work from home for part of the week. Workers say they save time and money because they no longer travel to the office every day. Some also find it easier to concentrate without the noise of busy colleagues. On the other hand, people living alone may feel lonely, and it can be difficult to separate work from family life. Managers worry that teams lose some of their creativity when they rarely meet. As a result, many firms now use a mixed model. Employees come to the office two or three days a week.

=== p042 | Traditional Crafts | culture | I
In many villages, traditional crafts are passed from parents to their children. Some families have made pottery, woven cloth or carved wood for hundreds of years. These crafts are not only a source of income but also an important part of local identity. Today, however, cheap factory goods have made it harder for craftspeople to earn a living. Some young people move to cities instead of learning the family skill. To help, several organizations sell handmade products online and teach new designs. Customers are often willing to pay more for something unique.

=== p043 | Space Exploration | science | A
Since the first satellite was launched in 1957, space exploration has advanced at a remarkable pace. Astronauts have walked on the moon, and robotic spacecraft have visited every planet in our solar system. Powerful telescopes now allow scientists to study galaxies that are billions of light years away. These missions have improved our understanding of the universe and produced technologies that we use every day, such as satellite navigation and weather forecasting. However, space programs are extremely expensive, and some people argue that the money should be spent on problems on Earth. Supporters respond that exploration inspires innovation and international cooperation.

=== p044 | A Day Without Electricity | story | I
Last month, a storm cut the power to our neighborhood for an entire day. At first, it felt like an adventure, and we searched the house for candles and batteries. Without the internet or television, my brothers and I played board games and read old comic books. My father cooked dinner on a small gas stove, and we ate together by candlelight. By the evening, our phones were dead and the refrigerator was getting warm. When the lights finally came back on, everyone cheered. Still, I secretly enjoyed the quiet day.

=== p045 | Learning a Second Language | education | A
Learning a second language requires patience, practice and a willingness to make mistakes. Research shows that regular exposure to the language is more effective than occasional long study sessions. Reading short texts, listening to podcasts and speaking with native speakers all help learners build vocabulary in meaningful contexts. Grammar is important, but it is easier to remember when it is learned through real examples. Many learners also find it useful to keep a notebook of new words and review it frequently. Over time, these small habits produce significant progress. Most importantly, successful learners do not give up when they feel frustrated.
