=== p016 | The Start of the Railway Age | history | I
In the early nineteenth century, most people traveled on foot, by horse or by boat. The first steam trains changed this almost overnight. Railways allowed factories to move coal and goods quickly, and they helped farmers send fresh food to the cities. As a result, many small towns grew into busy centers of trade. People who had never left their villages could now visit family far away. However, the new railways also brought noise, smoke and dangerous accidents. Within fifty years, the railway had changed the way millions of people lived and worked.

=== p017 | Why Sleep Matters | health | I
Scientists have learned a great deal about sleep in the last few decades. During sleep, the brain sorts the information it collected during the day and stores important memories. The body also repairs muscles and fights infections while we rest. People who do not get enough sleep often find it hard to concentrate, and they may feel anxious or angry more easily. Over time, poor sleep can increase the risk of serious illness. Experts recommend a regular bedtime and a dark, quiet room. Turning off phones an hour before bed can also help.

=== p018 | Smart Phones in the Classroom | technology | I
Many schools are now asking whether students should use phones in class. Supporters argue that phones give students quick access to dictionaries, maps and online lessons. Teachers can also use simple apps to check what the class has understood. On the other hand, phones can be a powerful distraction, and some students spend lessons sending messages to their friends. Several studies suggest that test results improve when phones are kept in bags. For this reason, some schools allow phones only during certain activities. The best rule may depend on the age of the students.

=== p019 | The Water Cycle | science | I
Water on Earth is always moving in a cycle. When the sun heats rivers, lakes and oceans, some of the water turns into vapor and rises into the air. As the vapor rises, it cools and forms tiny drops that gather into clouds. When the drops become too heavy, they fall back to the ground as rain or snow. Some of this water flows into rivers, while some soaks into the soil and is used by plants. Eventually, most of it returns to the sea. This cycle has continued for billions of years.

=== p020 | A Letter from the Front | history | I
During the war, soldiers wrote thousands of letters to their families at home. Most of these letters were short and simple, because paper was scarce and there was little time to write. The soldiers often tried to hide their fear and described the weather, the food or a funny moment with a friend. Families waited anxiously for each letter and read it many times. Some letters arrived weeks after they were written, and a few never arrived at all. Today, museums keep these letters because they show how ordinary people felt. They remind us that every soldier was also a son, a father or a friend.

=== p021 | Recycling in Our City | environment | I
Our city started a new recycling program two years ago. Every house now has three bins: one for paper, one for glass and metal, and one for food waste. At first, many people were confused and put things in the wrong bin. The city then sent a short guide to every family and held free classes at the library. Since then, the amount of trash that goes to the landfill has fallen by almost half. The money saved is being used to plant trees in the parks. Officials hope that other cities will follow the same plan.

=== p022 | Fixing Public Transport | city life | I
Many people in our city complain that the buses are slow and often late. Some areas have no bus service at all, so workers must walk long distances or pay for taxis. During peak hours, the buses are so crowded that passengers cannot find a seat. The city council has promised to buy new buses and create special lanes so that they can avoid traffic. It also plans to lower ticket prices for students and older people. If these changes work, more people may leave their cars at home. That would reduce both traffic and pollution.

=== p023 | The Secret of the Old Library | mystery | I
The old library had been closed for more than twenty years. When Maya finally opened the heavy door, a cloud of dust rose into the air. Tall shelves stood in long rows, and every book was covered in cobwebs. As she walked deeper into the room, the air grew colder and the light from the windows became faint. Suddenly she noticed a small door hidden behind one of the shelves. It was locked, but there was a strange symbol carved into the wood. Maya knew that she had to find the key.

=== p024 | Volunteering at the Animal Shelter | community | I
Last summer, I spent three weeks volunteering at an animal shelter near my home. The shelter takes care of dogs and cats that have been lost or abandoned. Every morning, I helped clean the cages and fill the bowls with fresh food and water. In the afternoon, I took the dogs for walks and played with the young cats. The work was tiring, but I learned a lot about patience and responsibility. Several animals found new homes while I was there. I plan to return next year.

=== p025 | Renewable Energy | energy | I
Countries around the world are trying to produce more electricity from renewable sources. Solar panels turn sunlight into power, and wind turbines use the movement of air. Unlike coal and oil, these sources will not run out, and they produce far less pollution. However, the sun does not always shine and the wind does not always blow. For this reason, engineers are developing better batteries to store energy for cloudy or calm days. The cost of solar power has fallen sharply in recent years. Many experts believe renewables will soon be the cheapest choice.

=== p026 | The Museum Visit | education | I
Our history class visited the national museum last week. The building is huge, so our teacher planned a route through the most important rooms. We started with ancient tools made of stone and bone, and then we saw pottery from the first villages. In another room, there were old coins, weapons and jewelry from different empires. My favorite part was a model of a royal palace with tiny furniture inside. At the end, each group had to describe one object to the class. I chose a small clay lamp that is three thousand years old.

=== p027 | Storm Warning | news | I
Weather officials have issued a severe storm warning for the coastal region. Strong winds and heavy rain are expected to arrive on Thursday evening. Residents are advised to stay indoors and avoid unnecessary travel. Schools near the coast will close, and some flights may be canceled. Authorities recommend that families prepare food, water and medicine in advance in case the power goes out. People who live in low areas should be ready to move to higher ground. Listen to the radio for the latest updates.

=== p028 | The Young Chef | story | I
Ever since she was a child, Lina loved to watch her grandmother cook. She learned how to choose fresh fish at the market and how much spice to add to each dish. When she was sixteen, she entered a cooking competition at her school. The other students made pizza and cakes, but Lina cooked a traditional fish curry. The judges tasted it and smiled, and one of them asked for the recipe. Lina won first prize. Her grandmother cried with pride when she heard the news.

=== p029 | Protecting Coral Reefs | environment | A
Coral reefs cover less than one percent of the ocean floor, yet they support about a quarter of all marine species. These complex ecosystems are formed by tiny animals that build hard skeletons over thousands of years. Unfortunately, reefs are extremely sensitive to changes in their environment. When the water becomes too warm, the coral loses its color and may eventually die. Pollution from farms and cities, as well as damaging fishing methods, adds further pressure. Scientists are now experimenting with ways to grow coral in protected nurseries. Without urgent action, many reefs could disappear within a generation.
