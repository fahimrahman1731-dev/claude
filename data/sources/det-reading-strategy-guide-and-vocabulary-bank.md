# DET Reading: Strategy Guide and Vocabulary Bank

Oct 7, 2026 · @Nur

## The reading section at a glance

Two of the four reading tasks reward the same skill: finishing a half-written word and spelling it exactly. Interactive Reading tests vocabulary through word choice instead. The table shows the 2026 format from Duolingo's own guides.

| Question type | Time | How many | What you do | Scoring |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Read and Select | 5 seconds per word | 15–18 words | Click Yes or No: is this a real English word? | Right or wrong |
| Fill in the Blanks | 20 seconds per sentence | 6–9 sentences | Type the missing second half of one word | Right or wrong; exact spelling |
| Read and Complete | 3 minutes per paragraph | 3–6 paragraphs | Type the missing second half of many words | Each word right or wrong; exact spelling |
| Interactive Reading | 7 or 8 minutes per passage | 2–3 passages, 6 questions each | Choose words, choose a sentence, highlight two answers, pick the main idea, pick a title | Right or wrong; highlights earn partial credit |

Sources: [DET Question Guide 2026](https://englishtest-static.duolingo.com/media/resources/Questions%20Guide%202026.pdf) and [DET Scoring Guide for Teachers 2026](<https://englishtest-static.duolingo.com/media/resources/DET_Scoring%20Guide%20for%20Teachers%20(2026).pdf>).

Three facts shape everything below:

- **Spelling is all or nothing.** One wrong letter scores zero in Fill in the Blanks and Read and Complete.
- **Every word counts alone.** Each gap in Read and Complete is scored separately, so a hard paragraph still holds easy points.
- **The test adapts.** Correct answers bring harder items, and harder words tell the test more about your level.

## Read and Complete

About 4 in 10 gaps are small grammar words, so fill those first and bank the easy points. I counted 1,368 gaps across the 76 practice paragraphs in Duolingo's official books ([Sample Questions](https://englishtest-static.duolingo.com/media/resources/GPN%20DET%20Sample%20Questions%20-%20Full.pdf), [Handbook](https://englishtest-static.duolingo.com/media/resources/GPN%20The%20DET%20Handbook.pdf), [Practice Questions Volume 3](https://englishtest-static.duolingo.com/media/resources/Practice%20Questions%20-%20Volume%203.pdf)). 42% were words like *the, and, to, of, in, was, it*, and those first five alone filled 19% of all gaps.

### Method: 3 minutes, about 18 gaps

1. Read the title, the first sentence and the last sentence. They are always complete and give you the topic.
2. Read the whole paragraph once without typing. Spend about 20 seconds.
3. First pass: fill every small word (*the, and, to, of, in, is, was, it, that, with*).
4. Second pass: fill the content words you recognize from the topic and the grammar.
5. Third pass: guess the hard ones. Each new word you fill makes the next one easier.
6. Last 20 seconds: reread and check every ending and every spelling.

### Tricks that come from how the task is built

- **Count the blanks.** The missing part is as long as the visible part, or one letter longer. So `t _ _` is a 3-letter word (*the, two, too*), `th _ _` has 4 letters (*that, this, they, them, then, than*) and `th _ _ _` has 5 (*their, there, these, those, three*).
- **Use the neighbors.** Gaps never sit side by side, so the words on both sides are complete. After *to* expect a base verb, after *has/have/had* expect *-ed*, after *is/was* expect *-ing* or *-ed*, after *a/an/the* expect a noun or adjective.
- **Look for repeats.** The answer, or another form of it, often appears in the title or elsewhere in the paragraph.
- **Type American spelling.** Duolingo's handbook says this task uses US spelling only: *center, theater, organize, traveling, color*.
- **Expect endings.** More than a third of the content words in the official paragraphs ended in *-s, -ed* or *-ing*. One wrong ending scores zero.
- **Rule out impossible answers.** Gaps are never names, acronyms, one-letter words, contractions or hyphenated words.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Type something in every gap | Leave a gap empty; an empty gap can never score |
| Start with the short words | Start at gap one and freeze on a hard word |
| Check plural and tense endings | Accept a word that fits the letters but breaks the grammar |
| Reread the full paragraph at the end | Spend more than 15 seconds on one gap in the first two passes |
| Stay silent with your eyes on the screen | Read aloud; speaking outside speaking tasks breaks the test rules |
| Use US spelling | Type *centre, theatre, organise* |

## Fill in the Blanks

One clue word in the sentence usually gives the answer: a synonym, an opposite, or a fixed partner. Difficulty climbs fast, from *bus* and *toy* to *unprecedented* and *mismanagement* in Duolingo's own practice sets.

### Method: 20 seconds per sentence

1. Read the whole sentence first. It has at most 20 words, so this takes 5 seconds.
2. Decide the part of speech from the neighbors: noun, verb, adjective or adverb.
3. Find the clue word and think of its partner: *balanced* → diet, *critical* → thinking, *renewable* → energy.
4. Count the blanks to confirm the length, then type.
5. Reread once with your word in place, then continue.

### Tricks

- **Partners decide most answers.** Official items include *a balanced diet, critical thinking, international cooperation, renewable energy, gravitational force, the fiscal year, ancient ruins* and *contract negotiations*.
- **Match the grammar ending.** "The cat jum \_ \_ \_ onto the table" needs past tense (*jumped*). "The dog barked lou \_ \_ \_" needs an adverb (*loudly*).
- **Long blanks mean a suffix.** Hard items usually end in *-tion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -able, -ive, -ous* or *-ally*. Bank 2 lists them.
- **Only four word types appear:** nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs. The answer is never *the* or *of* here.
- **Both spellings count here.** The official answer key accepts *summarize* or *summarise*.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Read to the end of the sentence before typing | Type the first word that matches the letters |
| Type a guess before time runs out | Let the 20 seconds end on an empty box |
| Check length, meaning and grammar | Forget the ending: *-s, -ed, -ing, -ly* |
| Learn each word with a partner word | Memorize single words with no example |

## Interactive Reading

The clock is shared: 7 or 8 minutes for all six questions on one passage, so pacing matters as much as vocabulary. Questions appear one at a time with no going back, and the next step reveals the correct words and sentence. Every test has at least one story and one informational passage, each about 70 to 140 words.

| Step | What you do | Suggested time | Key move |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Complete the Sentences | Pick a word for each of 3–10 blanks | 2:30 | Test each option for grammar, then partner word, then meaning |
| 2. Complete the Passage | Pick the missing sentence | 1:15 | Match linkers and pronouns to the sentences before and after |
| 3–4. Highlight the Answer (two questions) | Drag over the words that answer | 1:00 each | Find the question's keywords in the text; select the answer phrase only |
| 5. Identify the Idea | Pick the main idea | 0:45 | Choose the option that covers the whole passage |
| 6. Title the Passage | Pick a title | 0:30 | Match the idea you just chose |

### Complete the Sentences

- **Check the form first.** Traps often carry the right meaning in the wrong form: *hots* for *heats*, or *decade* after "the past hundred".
- **Partner words settle most blanks.** Official answers include *closely linked, plays a role, under threat, such as, for instance, breaks out, slows down* and *time management*.
- **Do the sure ones first.** You can fill the blanks in any order, but every blank needs a choice before you can continue.

### Complete the Passage

- Read the new second half carefully. The first half is the text you just completed.
- The right sentence continues the idea before the gap and sets up the sentence after it.
- Check linkers (*However, Therefore, For example, Furthermore*) and pronouns (*this, these, they, his*). Each must point to something that is really there.
- Preview a sentence inside the gap before you confirm it.

### Highlight the Answer

- The answer is always written in the passage. Scan for the question's keywords or their synonyms.
- Select the full phrase or clause that answers. Credit depends on how closely your selection matches, so one word is too little and two sentences are too much.
- Let the question word guide you: *why* → *because, due to, so*; *how* → *by, through*; *when* or *where* → a time or place phrase.
- Check your selection in the box under the question before you continue.

### Identify the Idea and Title the Passage

- Ask "What is this passage about?" and pick the option that covers the beginning, middle and end.
- Wrong options are usually one small detail, something the passage never said, or a much wider topic.
- The title should match the idea you just chose.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Check the timer after every step | Spend four minutes on the first step |
| Reuse what each step reveals | Reread the passage from zero at every step |
| Remove options that break the grammar first | Pick a word because it sounds advanced |
| Highlight the exact answer phrase | Highlight a whole sentence plus extra words |
| Answer all six questions | Run out of time before the last two questions |

## Vocabulary bank: how to use it

There is no fixed list to memorize: Duolingo's handbook says the question bank is so large that you are unlikely to meet the same question twice. So the six banks below target the kinds of words the format produces, starting with the ones that pay off fastest. Together they hold roughly 2,000 words, with some overlap.

| Bank | What it holds | Size | Based on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Small grammar words | about 190 | My count of 1,368 gaps in official paragraphs |
| 2 | Word endings | 26 endings | English word formation |
| 3 | Content words Duolingo blanked | 660 | Official practice books |
| 4 | Topic vocabulary | about 720 | My selection, by passage subject |
| 5 | Fill in the Blanks words with meanings | 223 | 103 official answers plus my selection |
| 6 | Spelling traps | about 240 | Common misspellings and US forms |

**How to drill:** cover the second half of each word with a card, type the whole word, then check. That copies the test exactly. Mark each miss and repeat only the misses the next day.

### Five spelling rules for endings

1. Final *-y* after a consonant becomes *-ies, -ied, -ier, -ily*: century → centuries, bury → buried, lively → livelier, easy → easily.
2. Drop the silent *-e* before *-ing*: make → making, gaze → gazing, create → creating.
3. Double the last consonant after a short stressed vowel: stop → stopped, sip → sipping, occur → occurred, begin → beginning.
4. Adjectives in *-ic* take *-ally*: geometric → geometrically.
5. *-ful* has one *l*, *-fully* has two: careful → carefully, beautiful → beautifully.

**Irregular past forms** from the official paragraphs: began, became, came, built, fought, led, sent, spent, took, won, made, felt, held, said, ran, sat, overthrew, gone, driven, broken, frozen, known, born. Add grew, knew, wrote, brought, thought, caught, taught, found, left, kept, paid, sold, told, stood, understood, chose, rose, fell, drew, flew.

## Bank 6: Spelling traps

A word you know but misspell scores zero, so drill these until your fingers type them without thought.

### American spelling for Read and Complete

Same-length pairs are the danger, because the British form fits the blanks too.

| Type this (American) | Not this (British) |
| --- | --- |
| center, theater, meter, liter, fiber | centre, theatre, metre, litre, fibre |
| organize, realize, recognize, summarize, apologize, analyze | organise, realise, recognise, summarise, apologise, analyse |
| defense, offense, license | defence, offence, licence |
| gray, skeptical, artifact | grey, sceptical, artefact |

Where the two forms differ in length, the blank count shows you the American one: color, favorite, neighbor, behavior, honor, labor, harbor, flavor, humor, traveling, traveled, canceled, modeling, jewelry, program, catalog, judgment, fulfill, enrollment, skillful.

## Seven-day plan

Banks 1 to 3 come first because they are built from Duolingo's own material. Do every practice item on a keyboard and against the clock.

| Day | Learn | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Bank 1 look-up table; Bank 2 spelling rules | 3 Read and Complete paragraphs, untimed |
| 2 | Bank 3 Level 1, and Level 2 from A to M | 5 paragraphs at 3 minutes each |
| 3 | Bank 3 Level 2 from N to W, and Level 3 | 5 paragraphs; 15 Fill in the Blanks |
| 4 | Bank 5 official table; Bank 6 | 30 Fill in the Blanks at 20 seconds each |
| 5 | Bank 5 second table; first 7 topics of Bank 4 | 3 Interactive Reading sets at 7 minutes each |
| 6 | Last 7 topics of Bank 4 | Duolingo's free practice test |
| 7 | Every word you marked as a miss | 5 paragraphs and 2 Interactive Reading sets |

The practice paragraphs are in the official books linked under Sources. Your Practice Hub still has unfinished Read and Complete and Interactive Reading sets to use as well.
