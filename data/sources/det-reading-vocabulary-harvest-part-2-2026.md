# DET Reading Vocabulary Harvest, Part 2 (2026): Fill in the Blanks, Read and Complete, Interactive Reading

The best new source is the official **DET Practice Questions Volume 4 (2026)**, which has an answer key.\[1\] From it I harvested every answer word: 45 Fill in the Blanks words, about 600 content-word gaps across 30 Read and Complete paragraphs, and 10 Interactive Reading sets with all trap options. I could not access Volume 5. Third-party sources add a few hundred more words. Duolingo's own research tells you what kind of words to study. Park, Cardwell & Naismith, "Assessing Vocabulary on the Duolingo English Test" (Duolingo Research Report, 2024, updated 2025), says Fill in the Blanks targets come from a "corpus across four different parts of speech (adjectives, adverbs, nouns, and verbs) using lemma frequency". Items use collocations and synonym/antonym clues, and at the top level they move toward long, Latin-based abstract nouns.

## TL;DR
- **Biggest new official source:** DET Practice Questions Volume 4 (2026) is fully harvested below (Fill in the Blanks, Read and Complete, and Interactive Reading with distractors). Volume 5 is confirmed to exist in Duolingo's 2026 Question Guide, but neither I nor a dedicated search agent could open it. Volumes 1 and 2 do not appear to exist under that name.
- **What Duolingo's research says about word choice:** Fill in the Blanks target words are picked from a large English corpus by lemma frequency, across nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs. Every test taker gets at least one item with a synonym or antonym clue and at least one collocation item. Harder items use rarer, longer, Latin- or Greek-origin words. So the most useful study is B2–C1 academic and general words *with their collocations*, not rare "GRE" words.
- **Honest size limit:** the evidence-based new words here come to roughly 750 distinct items, not 2,000. To reach 2,000+ honestly, combine this harvest with your existing 763-word bank. Then add one of two lists. The first is the Oxford 5000 extension, which Oxford Learner's Dictionaries describes as "an additional 2,000 words that are aligned to the CEFR, guiding advanced learners at B2-C1 level". The second is the Academic Vocabulary List (Gardner & Davies, Applied Linguistics, 2014), which Duolingo's vocabulary paper cites: 3,000 lemmas drawn from a 120-million-word academic part of the Corpus of Contemporary American English (COCA). A best-estimate topic list is included and clearly labeled.

## Key Findings

1. **Volume 4 is ordered easy → hard, and that order shows how the adaptive test raises vocabulary level.** The book itself says questions within each type go from easier to harder.\[1\] Its Fill in the Blanks answers start with 3–5 letter A1 nouns (shoes, water, rain, dog). They end with 9–13 letter abstract nouns and -ing forms (collaboration, deliberation, methodology, complexities, forecasting, aspirations).\[1\] If you are aiming for a high score, items 33–45 show the level you will meet.
2. **Fill in the Blanks is built on word frequency plus a context clue.** Duolingo's vocabulary white paper (2025) says target words come from a corpus "across four different parts of speech (adjectives, adverbs, nouns, and verbs) using lemma frequency". Sentences come in five genres (literary, textbook, news, personal writing, conversation).\[2\] The test guarantees at least one synonym/antonym-clue item and at least one collocation item.
3. **Interactive Reading traps follow a clear pattern in Volume 4.** Wrong options are usually one of three kinds. (a) The right idea in the wrong word form (unhappy for anxiety, enjoyable for praise, interesting for captivated). (b) A near-synonym that breaks a collocation (efficient vs. *highly effective*; evidences/proof vs. *clues*). (c) A rare, impressive-looking word (miasma, hullabaloo, hokum, miniscule). In all 10 sets, the rare "fancy" word was never the answer.
4. **Read and Complete gaps are mostly ordinary words with grammar endings.** In Volume 4, many content-word answers carry endings: -ed (walked, stumbled, flickered, rationed, delivered), -ing (carrying, scanning, whispering), and plural -s (valves, lungs, landfills, communities).\[1\] Getting the ending right from tense and number matters as much as knowing the word.
5. **Topics match Duolingo's stated domains.** Volume 4 Read and Complete covers:
   - geography (the Nile) and the human body (the heart);
   - history (the Industrial Revolution, wartime letters);
   - a science procedure (a lab experiment);
   - the environment (recycling, renewable energy, storms);
   - city life (transport, a festival, a bakery) and technology (privacy);
   - a large share of literary fiction (about 12 of 30 passages).
   The 2026 Technical Manual says Read and Complete passages include "fiction (e.g., colloquial narratives), news articles, and textbook passages".\[2\]\[3\]
6. **British words and spellings still appear in official material outside Read and Complete.** Volume 4 Interactive Reading uses *kerb, flat, pram, dressing gown*, and Read and Complete passage 23 shows *grey* as a complete word. The Fill in the Blanks key accepts *organizing / organising*. Only the words you type in Read and Complete must be American.\[4\]

## Findings on Word Selection

- **CEFR range:** In the same TACL paper (2020), "a panel of linguistics PhDs with ESL teaching experience" compiled "an inventory of 6,823 English words labeled by CEFR level, mostly in the B1/B2 range". Scores are reported across CEFR A1–C2. Volume 4 matches this: FIB items 1–10 are A1, items 11–32 are A2–B1 everyday words, and items 33–45 are B2–C1 academic nouns.
- **Word origin and length:** The TACL paper found that advanced words "are rarer and mostly have Greco-Latin etymologies", while basic words are common and Anglo-Saxon.\[13\] In Volume 4, the 11 hardest FIB answers average about 11 letters; the 10 easiest average about 4.5.
- **Corpus:** Duolingo research uses COCA (an American corpus) for vocabulary item features (BanditCAT/AutoIRT paper).\[15\] The vocabulary white paper cites general words plus academic words and the Academic Vocabulary List (Gardner & Davies, 2014).\[16\] That is the strongest official hint about which list to use.
- **Interactive Reading generation:** Park et al. (DRR-22-02, June 16, 2022) state that "All reading passages and accompanying items (including the stems and the distractors…) are automatically generated by Generative Pre-trained Transformer 3 (GPT-3)". They then go through three review stages: automated screening, light human editing, and a human fairness/bias review. Hidden words are chosen as "the most likely word by BERT", and distractors are also chosen with BERT likelihood plus lexical and syntactic analysis. So the right answer is the most *natural* word in context, which is why fixed collocations decide most items.
- **Adaptivity:** the adaptive test picks items whose difficulty matches its current estimate of your ability. A stronger test taker therefore sees longer, rarer, more abstract words in Fill in the Blanks, and literary or academic Read and Complete passages (like Volume 4 passages 15–30).
- **Best-fit lists by task:**
  - *Fill in the Blanks:* Oxford 3000 → Oxford 5000 (an additional 2,000 words at B2–C1), plus the Academic Collocation List, because every test includes a collocation item. The Academic Collocation List is by Ackermann & Chen (2013, Journal of English for Academic Purposes), built from the Pearson International Corpus of Academic English. It contains "2,469 of the most frequent and useful collocations which occur in written academic English".
  - *Read and Complete:* New General Service List / Oxford 3000 for the high-frequency core, plus practice with word endings.
  - *Interactive Reading:* Academic Vocabulary List / AWL plus collocations, since the right option is usually the most typical collocate.

## Additional Strategy Points (new or conflicting only)

1. **Fix the ending from tense and number before guessing the word.** This is the official Volume 4 Read and Complete tip: "Pay attention to the tense of the sentences".
2. **In Interactive Reading you do not need to reread the passage for each sub-question** (official Volume 4 tip).\[1\]
3. **Look for the synonym/antonym clue.** Every test has at least one Fill in the Blanks item built on a synonym or antonym, and one built on a collocation (vocabulary white paper). Watch for *unlike, rather than, not… but, despite, and also*.
4. **In Complete the Sentences, distrust the rare, showy word.** In Volume 4, miasma, hullabaloo, hokum, miniscule, profuse and atrium were all wrong.\[1\] Also reject wrong word forms (unhappy, enjoyable, interesting, showing) and uncountable traps (evidences).
5. **Conflict, US vs UK spelling in Fill in the Blanks:** Arno says only American spelling is accepted there.\[7\] The official 2026 guides and the Volume 4 key (organizing/organising) say both are accepted.\[1\] Trust the official sources; only Read and Complete is US-only.\[4\]
6. **Conflict, number of Interactive Reading sets:** the 2026 Question Guide says 2–3 sets, and Volume 4 (2026) says 2 sets.\[1\] The 2026 Technical Manual describes one narrative and one expository passage (one 7-minute, one 8-minute). Plan for 2, be ready for 3.
7. **Conflict, number of options and blanks:** KSE Academy says 3–4 options and abroadcube says 5–8 blanks.\[10\]\[17\] Official sources say 3–10 blanks. Duolingo's Interactive Reading paper (DRR-22-02) says test takers "choose the most appropriate word for the blank from five options", and Volume 4 shows 4–6 options per blank.
8. **Third-party format errors:** Gurully's mid-word gaps and Lume calling FIB "Read and Complete" do not match the official format. Use them for words only.
9. **Possible official key error:** Volume 4 R&C 14 ("la_ _" keyed as "air"). If your answer fits the letters and the meaning, trust your own logic.
